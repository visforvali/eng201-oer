## Welcome to ENG 201!

<img src="https://visforvali.github.io/eng201-oer/assets/gitbook/images/drmani.png" alt="Picrew profile of Vyshali, an Eelam Tamil woman" width="25%" style="float: left; margin: 15px; size: 12px; border-radius: 50%;">

Hello! I'm Dr. Mani, and I'll be your professor for ENG 201: Writing in the Disciplines this semester. I'm an Eelam Tamil American chronically ill writer, scholar, artist, and designer. I've taught writing and rhetoric, media studies, disability studies, and cultural studies for ~20 years. I have a PhD in Communication, Information, and Media from Rutgers and an MFA in Creative Writing from Columbia. I'm interested in digital multimodal composition, disability justice movements (mask up 😷 for your community!), and body mods, and our self-appointed TA for the class is my Siberian cat, Athena (<a href="https://visforvali.github.io/eng201-oer/assets/cat_tax.jpg" target="_blank">obligatory cat tax</a>). My server alias is Flame Thief, a reference to *Hades 2*, and my hill to die on is that writing *is* thinking, not just the menial task that happens after the thinking is done. 

I believe in critical, accessible, social justice pedagogies. What this means for you is: a flexible, responsive course architecture, lessons, and professor-student communication; equitable discussions, assignments, and grading schema; and an expectation that you'll be actively involved in reviewing the course, creating community, and learning. You can always reach out to me with a concern by mentioning ("@") me on Discord, DMing me directly, or sending me an email. 

This section of ENG 201: Writing in the Disciplines is offered by the Dept. of English, Writing, and Cultural Studies at Pace University - Pleasantville (PLV). Our core writing program is carefully scaffolded, and this "writing in the disciplines" (WID) composition class is unique in that it teaches you how to both:

1. Conduct qualitative research, and
2. Enter the distinct discourse community of your discipline. 

ENG 201 is designed to build on previously learned writing skills, using qualitative research to help you develop argumentation, analytical skills, advanced research skills using primary and secondary sources, and the patient, curious, dogged mentality needed for many kinds of writing. 

> ##### If You're Working on a Thesis
> **ENG 201 is *not* aimed at helping you write a thesis and is *only* relevant to your thesis in terms of writing craft, not content!** This course will teach you to emulate the disciplinary conventions of your field in preparation for future scholarly and professional texts you'll write, using qualitative research as the vehicle for your learning. If you can apply discipline-specific moves across genres of writing, you can easily employ them in the writing tasks of your field.
{: .block-tip }

ENG 201 centers research and method to support students in exploring contemporary field-related concepts through research writing that synthesizes primary and secondary sources. Our low- and high-stakes writing work is designed so you can engage with your field; understand how information is developed, determined, presented, and valued; and practice writing for those communicative expectations. And because writing is a social activity, you'll also work closely in small groups (to be assigned by Week 3) in asynchronous discussions and peer workshops. 

This section of ENG 201 is designed for the PLV core writing program and has similar requirements regarding participation and collaborative work, assignments, drafting and revision, and grading policies. It presumes prior content knowledge and writing skill &mdash; in a basic sense at least, no content mastery required &mdash; from ENG 120, including the ability to: recognize and define discourse communities; engage in discourse analysis; understand the rhetorical situation; close-read popular, literary, and scholarly articles; use library databases; and document your sources. 

If you're unfamiliar with the format and expectations of PLV core writing courses, or if you've gotten rusty since you took ENG 120, you may initially feel lost. And that's okay! No one masters anything in a single semester, anyway. Whether or not you took a PLV section of ENG 110 or 120, you can succeed in this course! Just be aware of our actual course expectations, and don't base your understanding of core writing courses or writing in the disciplines on anecdotal information about what this course has been like for others.

### A Warning About Async Courses

This is an ASYNC section, meaning we will never meet synchronously. Asynchronous classes are fully remote and self-paced, meaning you aren't required to synchronously interact with your instructor or classmates at a set meeting time. If you're taking this async section to be able to maximize work and credit hours, be advised: **While async courses permit greater flexibility for arranging a tight schedule, they are always more time- and labor-intensive.** The workload is equivalent to a sync 3-hr credit course, but it can feel like more than that without set meeting times and real-time oversight from professors. It's easy to lose focus or fall behind in async courses, especially if you have a busy schedule already. You're responsible for dedicating enough time to the course and remaining motivated and on track even when life, work, and other classes get in the way. 

All of the coursework is already available to you on this website and our Discord server is already set up for asynchronous participation, so you can pace yourself appropriately. That said, I strongly recommend **adhering to the calendar as much as possible**: i.e., don't work too far ahead, and try not to fall too far behind.

Starting in Week 2 (9/7-9/11), I'll be available for synchronous coffee chats &mdash; a friendlier name for office hours! &mdash; on Tue 2-5 and Wed 1-3. I'm not allowed to mandate sync chats for async courses, but former students have said that regularly meeting with me one-on-one really helped them succeed in the course. You can reserve coffee chats with me on [Zcal](https://zcal.co/drmani){:target="_blank"}.

**Because this is an ASYNC section, you'll follow an individualized workflow, but the planned weekly workflow starts on Sun and ends on Sat.** Your typical weekly workflow will look like this:

1. **Sun**: Check the Course Calendar for an overview of the work due in that week.
2. **Sun/Mon**: Begin the weekly module.
3. **Tue**: Share your [question post](https://visforvali.github.io/eng201-oer/pages/assignments/#weekly-question--answer-posts){:target="_blank"} in #🙋classroom on Discord by Tue 11:59pm.
3. **Thu/Fri**: Complete all readings and exercises linked and described in each module and listed on the Calendar, whether they're "for you only" practice exercises or assignment submissions.
4. **Fri**: Finish the weekly module and share your [answer post](../pages/assignments/){:target="_blank"} in #🙋classroom by Fri 11:59pm.
5. **Sat**: Review my responses in #🙋classroom to assess whether or not you're on track with your learning, and book coffee chats if needed for the upcoming week. When qualitative research project drafts are due, you'll usually submit them by Sat 11:59pm.

**This pattern may vary from week to week. Check the Calendar every Sun for the exact schedule of assigned readings and written assignments.**

### About this OER

This course website serves as an open educational resource (OER) for ENG 201, brings together course-specific documents and weekly modules I created, which contain links to open-access or freely available resources designed to help you improve your existing critical reading, critical writing, and writing-in-the-disciplines (WID) skills. It compiles text-based lessons, linked readings, "how-to" instructions, audiovisual content, instructional demos, practice exercises, and model student papers from students at Pace and elsewhere. All these materials can be read linearly and revisited as needed, depending on your learning needs (though I recommend you follow the recommended workflow as much as possible).

> ##### Note
> Out of respect for your selection of an asynchronous modality, and to reduce everyone’s screen fatigue, I have intentionally kept audiovisual content to a minimum. While some audiovisual instructional resources have been included, you shouldn't need to block out too much synchronous time to watch them. This website is responsive and can be read on a computer, tablet, or phone, and &mdash; along with the Discord smartphone app &mdash; lends itself much better to "on-the-go" work, collaboration, and communication than Classes or Outlook.
{: .block-tip }

Each module is designed to stand alone and with other course materials and identifies important general and discipline-specific methods for research writing and explains how you can practice them in your ENG 201 qualitative research project. As their titles indicate, these modules will guide you through different aspects of the research process &mdash; such as developing research questions; doing secondary and primary research; organizing, synthesizing, and presenting data; and the discipline-specific nature of communication expectations.

There are also appendices with content aimed at particular academic specializations. You'll be directed to these appendices throughout the semester, but I encourage you to reference the ones relevant to your discipline whenever you need to.

### Classes LMS

Classes is unconducive to real learning since it encourages a grades-oriented mindset of "let me get my work done and GTFO with minimal interaction." Additionally, generative AI (GenAI) has been forced into course learning management systems (LMS) like Classes, where it fabricates information about your progress, steals your writing (and increases false positives in plagiarism detection, putting you at risk of accusations of academic integrity violations), and manipulates people into believing they can outsource any part of their thought process without proportionally atrophying their brains.

To avoid that circus, you'll access course content on this website (though certain files are x-posted to Classes) and participate almost exclusively on Discord.

You'll use Classes primarily to submit drafts of your qualitative research project in assignment dropboxes in [Classes\Assignments](https://classes.pace.edu){:target="_blank"} and to review your scores in [Classes\Gradebook](https://classes.pace.edu){:target="_blank"}. For privacy and security reasons, the invitation link to our Discord server is posted to [Classes\Content\Syllabus](https://classes.pace.edu){:target="_blank"}.

# It's Okay Not to Know!

You might not be used to hearing this, let alone believing it, from a professor &mdash; but this course works best when we all show up as our authentic, imperfect, selves, ready to make mistakes and fail at things &mdash; when we treat this class like a space to chill and learn, not a space where you anxiously await judgment or avoid it by staying silent. We all have things to contribute, and we'll all get lost and trip up along the way. Nurturing your curiosity despite those errors and learning to productively, vocally engage with discomfort is what writing and research are all about.

ENG 201 courses are made up of students and instructors who come from very different disciplinary backgrounds. Each person in the classroom has a different level of familiarity and comfort with different disciplinary conventions: for instance, a business major will know something about writing business reports but may know less about writing an anthropology paper or engineering report. Similarly, I possess expertise in writing, medicine, communication and media studies, and the fine arts, and I'm familiar with legal writing and science writing, but I may not be expert in the writing conventions of your discipline.

This course isn't meant to "test" your knowledge of research or discipline-specific writing. You're not supposed to know everything about writing in your discipline right now, and that's OK! 

ENG 201 as a class best approached as a community of learners. I'll guide you through the process of writing a qualitative research paper and will help you read to identify disciplinary conventions and practice those conventions in your own writing. And as we work together this semester, I hope you can accept that real, productive learning begins with discomfort. That means accepting that you don't already know everything; being responsible for asking questions in our classroom text chat; accepting that you'll reveal the gaps in your knowledge and make mistakes in order to learn; staying calm in the face of discomfort and anxiety (avoid "I'm going to fail" panic or fury); and finding time as your schedule permits to have sync coffee chats with me if you're confused.
